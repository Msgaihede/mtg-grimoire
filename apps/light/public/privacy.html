MTG Grimoire privacy policy

Effective 18 October 2026. This covers the MTG Grimoire apps for Android, for the web at mtg-grimoire.app, and for Windows and Linux.

MTG Grimoire is made by Markus Seerup. It is a tool for keeping track of a Magic: The Gathering collection. It has no accounts, shows no advertising, and contains no analytics, tracking or crash-reporting code.

What stays on your device

Everything you make in the app is stored on your own device, in the app's own storage (on a computer, a data folder that belongs to the app): your collection, decks, wishlist, notes, labels and settings. So are the card database, the card pictures the app has shown you, the card scanner's files if you downloaded them, and a log of errors the app ran into. The app does not send any of this to its maker.

On Android the app is excluded from Google's device backup, so your collection is not copied to your Google account. In a browser, the same data is kept in that browser's storage for mtg-grimoire.app.

What the app asks other services for

The app downloads card data, card pictures and prices from the services below, directly from your device, and every connection it makes is encrypted (HTTPS). Each of them sees your IP address and the request itself, as any website you visit does, and may log it under its own policy. The Android and desktop apps identify themselves as MTGGrimoire with their version number; in a browser, your browser identifies itself as it always does. None of these requests contains your collection, your decks or anything that identifies you to MTG Grimoire, with two exceptions, each described below: sync, which carries your changes encrypted so that only your own devices can read them, and the last row of the table, which carries camera pictures you chose to send. Which pictures are asked for follows which cards you look at.

The camera

The app asks for the camera the first time you use it to scan a pairing code or, where the app offers it, a card. Pictures from the camera are examined on your device. They are not kept and are not sent anywhere, with two exceptions, and each takes a press of yours: the desktop app's card scanner has a control that saves one picture to a folder on your computer, and the card scanner's Send, described next. So that there is something to send, the card scanner holds the last few seconds of what its camera saw — at most twenty pictures, none older than four seconds — in memory while it is scanning. Each is dropped as newer ones arrive, none is written to storage, and they are let go when the scanner stops. If you press Send, the pictures it shows you are held until you close it.

Sending a scan

Where a Patreon membership is connected to your sync group, the card scanner has a Send control; without one the control is not shown, and nothing in this section happens. It is how you can show MTG Grimoire's maker a card the scanner read wrongly, or read well, so the scanner can be tested against it and improved. The scanner makes up its mind over a couple of seconds and not from one picture, so a scan is the short run of pictures it was looking at just before you pressed: at least five and at most twenty, usually about two seconds and never more than four. Nothing is sent until you press Send and then confirm: the app first shows you every one of those pictures, and you choose which card and which finish they show. It is all of them or none — the app never sends a single picture, and there is no way to send only some.

What is sent is:

  • those pictures, as the scanner was given them — usually smaller than the camera's own, and showing whatever was in front of the camera in those seconds, which is why the app asks you to look through them first;
  • the card and finish you chose;
  • what the scanner itself had read: the card it had settled on, if it had settled on one, how sure it was, which scanning mode was on, how many cards its data held, and which sets and release dates you had narrowed it to;
  • for each picture: how long before your press it was taken, its size, and what the scanner made of it — whether it found a card and was holding steady on it, how sure it was, whether it had settled on one, and whether it also looked at a larger copy of that picture (the larger copy is not sent);
  • the app's version, and whether it was sent from the Android app, the web app or a desktop app.

It is stored on Cloudflare, in MTG Grimoire's own account, with the time it arrived, where only its maker can read it, and it is used only to test and improve the card scanner. It is not published and is not passed to anyone else. Nothing stored with it says who sent it: no name, no account, no device or group identifier, and not your IP address. So that the server can check that a membership is connected, the request carries the short-lived token the app already uses to sync, which names the membership's Patreon user identifier, your sync group and your device. It is read while the request is being answered and is not stored, with the scan or anywhere else. Like every request the app makes, this one reaches the server from your IP address, and the Android and desktop apps name themselves and their version as they always do; the address is used while the request is being answered, to limit how often one address may send, and is not kept. This server keeps no log of requests.

When a scan has been sent the app shows a reference for it. A sent scan is kept until the scanner no longer has a use for it, or until you ask for it to be deleted.

Sync between your devices

Sync is optional. If you pair two or more installs of MTG Grimoire, they exchange changes through a relay server that MTG Grimoire runs on Cloudflare.

Changes are encrypted on your device with a key that only your paired devices hold. The relay cannot read them, and neither can its maker. Beside each encrypted change the relay stores what it needs to deliver it:

  • a random identifier for the device that sent it and one for your group of devices — neither is your name, your phone's name or an advertising identifier;
  • the time the change was made and the time it arrived;
  • for each device in the group, when the relay first and last heard from it;
  • your group's sync key, sealed so that only your devices can open it;
  • how far each device has read, a one-way check value for the group's key, and a list of the devices that have left the group.

The relay's database does not store device names, e-mail addresses or IP addresses. Your IP address is used while a request is being answered, to limit how often one address may ask. Cloudflare, which hosts the relay, also keeps a log of each request to it, in MTG Grimoire's own Cloudflare account, for up to seven days: it holds the address that was asked for, which contains your group's identifier, and the request's headers, which include your IP address. When the relay runs into a fault it adds a line about the fault to the same log; a fault while a membership is being checked names that membership's Patreon user id. MTG Grimoire's maker can read that log and uses it only to find faults.

A change is deleted from the relay once every device in the group has received it and it is 30 days old, and a device the relay has not heard from in 90 days is dropped from its records; the relay does both the next time a device in the group syncs. A group whose devices have all stopped syncing keeps its last records until its membership ends — the stored changes are deleted then — or until you ask. The short-lived data used while two devices pair stops working after ten minutes and is removed by an hourly clean-up.

If a Patreon membership is connected to your sync group

The Android app cannot connect a Patreon membership. If one has been connected to your sync group from another install of MTG Grimoire, the relay asks Patreon whether it is active and stores: your Patreon user identifier, the membership's state, a token that lets the relay ask Patreon again later, which group of devices it covers, which device connected it, and when the record was made and last checked. It does not ask Patreon for your name, your e-mail address or what you pay. It does learn from Patreon when a payment has failed, because sync then continues for seven days. Patreon also notifies the relay when a membership changes; the relay reads the user identifier and the state from that notice and keeps nothing else. This record is kept after a membership ends, until you ask for it to be deleted.

Sharing a collection

The desktop app can publish a read-only snapshot of a collection folder to a link. A published snapshot is stored on Cloudflare, readable by anyone who has the link, and contains the title and name you typed, the names of the folder and its sub-folders, the cards and their values, and their condition and language if you chose to include them. It never contains purchase prices, acquisition dates or private notes. It stays until you withdraw it from the app, and its link stops working if the membership it was published under ends. After you withdraw it, a record that it existed stays, with the title and the name you typed. The Android app and the web app cannot publish.

The Discord integration

The desktop apps can let you look up your cards from Discord, with slash commands that work in any server or chat. It is off until you switch it on in Settings, under Integrations, and it is offered only where a Patreon membership is connected to your sync group. The Android app and the web app do not have it.

Switching it on opens Discord's own sign-in page in your browser, where you approve MTG Grimoire; the app never sees your Discord password. The integration's service, which MTG Grimoire runs on Cloudflare at discord.mtg-grimoire.app, then keeps your Discord user id and your Discord user name, and a one-way check value of a random secret your desktop app holds, so that only that app can change the link.

Signing in shares nothing else. The next step is a separate choice, and the app asks you to confirm it: it adds a read-only device, run by MTG Grimoire on Cloudflare, to your sync group. Like any device in the group it holds the group's key, so — unlike the relay, which stores only what it cannot read — it holds a copy it can decrypt of everything that syncs: your collection, your wishlist and your decks, including your notes on them and the prices you paid. It uses that copy only to answer the commands you send it from Discord. When you use a command, Discord sends it to the service with your Discord user id, and the answer goes back to Discord to be shown where you typed it. The device cannot change anything in your group, and it takes one of the group's five device places.

Switching the integration off removes that device from your sync group, which changes the group's key so that it can read nothing synced afterwards, and deletes its copy, and deletes the link with your Discord id and name. Removing MTG Grimoire from your Discord account is done in Discord, under User Settings, then Authorized Apps.

Removing your data

  • Everything on a phone is removed by uninstalling the app; in a browser, by clearing the site's data; on a computer, by deleting the app's data folder. Settings also has a reset.
  • A device leaves a sync group from Settings, under Sync; any device in the group can remove another. Removing a device stops it receiving anything new.
  • Switching off the Discord integration, in Settings under Integrations, removes its device from your sync group and deletes its copy of your cards, and your Discord id and name. To remove MTG Grimoire from your Discord account, use Discord's User Settings, then Authorized Apps.
  • To have a scan you sent deleted, write to the address below with the reference the app showed when it was sent. Nothing stored with a scan says who sent it, so the reference is the only way it can be found.
  • To have a group's records, a membership's record or a shared snapshot deleted from the relay, write to the address below and include the device identifier shown in Settings, under Sync — the line of letters and digits beneath this device's name. It is how the right records are found.

Children

MTG Grimoire is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone.
